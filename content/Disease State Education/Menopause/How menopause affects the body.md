## Symptoms you notice
- **Hot flashes and night sweats** (vasomotor symptoms): the most common reason women seek care
- **Sleep, mood and thinking:** broken sleep, irritability, low mood or anxiety, and "brain fog". Memory concerns usually improve after the transition
- **Genitourinary:** vaginal dryness, pain with sex, urinary urgency and recurrent infections. Unlike hot flashes, these **worsen over time** without treatment
- **Skin, hair, joints:** drier skin, thinning hair, joint aches

## Changes you do not notice
- **Bone:** the fastest bone loss of life happens in the first 5 to 7 years after menopause
- **Heart and metabolism:** LDL cholesterol rises, fat shifts to the abdomen, and insulin resistance increases. Weight gain itself is driven more by age and lifestyle than by menopause

## What helps
- **Hormone therapy** is the most effective treatment for hot flashes and also prevents bone loss. Benefits are greatest when started before 60 or within 10 years of menopause
- **Vaginal estrogen** treats genitourinary symptoms with very little absorption into the blood
- **Non-hormonal options** for hot flashes include fezolinetant and elinzanetant (neurokinin receptor blockers), some antidepressants, gabapentin and CBT
- Strength training, protein, calcium and vitamin D for bone and muscle; check lipids and blood pressure

## Clinician teaching points
- FDA began removing the class boxed warning on cardiovascular disease, breast cancer and dementia in November 2025; the endometrial cancer warning remains for systemic estrogen-alone therapy in women with a uterus. Counsel from the updated product labels and The Menopause Society statements
- Separate symptom treatment (vasomotor, GSM) from prevention (bone) when counselling on duration
