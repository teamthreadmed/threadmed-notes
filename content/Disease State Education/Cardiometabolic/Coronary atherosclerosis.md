## What the picture shows
The heart muscle gets its own blood supply from the **coronary arteries** running over its surface. Two are labelled: the **right coronary artery** and the **left anterior descending** artery (also called the anterior interventricular artery), which feeds the front wall of the heart.

The box zooms into the LAD. Inside the artery wall sits a **plaque**: a pocket of cholesterol, inflammatory cells and scar tissue. Its soft, fatty core is covered by a thin cap.

## Why it matters
Most heart attacks do not come from a slowly narrowing pipe. They happen when the cap over a plaque **cracks or erodes**, the body treats it like a wound, and a **blood clot** forms on top. The clot can block the artery within minutes. That is why a plaque that was not causing symptoms can still cause a heart attack.

## What drives plaque
- **LDL cholesterol and the other apoB-carrying particles** are the cause, not just a marker. The more of them, and the longer they circulate, the more plaque
- **Lipoprotein(a)**, an inherited particle, adds risk on top of LDL
- Smoking, high blood pressure, diabetes and inflammation damage the wall and speed the process

## What helps
- **Lower LDL, earlier and for longer.** Statins first; ezetimibe, PCSK9-targeted therapy or bempedoic acid when more lowering is needed
- Lowering LDL shrinks the soft core and thickens the cap, making plaque less likely to rupture
- Stop smoking; control blood pressure and glucose
- Antiplatelet therapy, when your clinician recommends it, makes a clot less likely to form on a cracked plaque

## Clinician teaching points
- The 2026 ACC/AHA/Multisociety guideline replaces the 2018 cholesterol guideline, restores LDL-C and non-HDL-C goals, uses PREVENT-ASCVD for risk, and recommends measuring Lp(a) at least once in all adults
- Goals: LDL-C <100 mg/dL (borderline/intermediate risk), <70 (high risk or ASCVD), <55 (very high-risk ASCVD); non-HDL-C 30 mg/dL higher. Confirm the exact category definitions against the full text before quoting them to patients
- Use the image to separate **stenosis** (symptoms on exertion) from **rupture plus thrombus** (acute events); this is why a "clean" stress test does not mean no plaque
