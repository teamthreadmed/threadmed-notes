## What the picture shows
Intravascular ultrasound (IVUS) looks at a heart artery from the inside, as a cross-section. The pink centre is where blood flows; the orange ring is **plaque** in the wall. **Plaque burden** is the share of the vessel taken up by plaque.

On the left, plaque shrinks over a year of treatment. On the right, it grows.

## Lower LDL, less plaque
![Change in percent atheroma volume against achieved LDL-C across IVUS trials](/attachments/ivus-ldl-pav.svg)

Each dot is one treatment arm of a clinical trial. Across trials, the lower the LDL-C people reached, the more their plaque shrank. Plaque **grew** at an average LDL around 110 mg/dL and **shrank** once LDL was roughly below 70.

## What this means for you
- Plaque is not fixed. Lowering LDL enough can shrink it and, as importantly, makes the remaining plaque more stable and less likely to rupture
- The size of the change on IVUS is small (around 1%), but it tracks with fewer heart attacks in outcome trials
- This is the reason the 2026 guideline goals are <70 mg/dL for high risk and <55 mg/dL for very high risk

## Clinician teaching points
- Plotted values are the trial arms as summarised in the PRECISE-IVUS figure: REVERSAL (prava 40 vs atorva 80), ASTEROID, SATURN, PRECISE-IVUS split by stable disease and ACS. The fit (non-ACS arms) gives r² ≈ 0.92; check against the original papers before quoting numbers
- Lower still works: GLAGOV (evolocumab on statin, achieved LDL-C ~36 mg/dL) showed regression in a majority; PACMAN-AMI and HUYGENS added cap thickening and lipid-core reduction on OCT/NIRS
- Use with the [[Coronary atherosclerosis]] figure: regression is mainly lipid-core shrinkage and cap thickening, not a wider lumen
